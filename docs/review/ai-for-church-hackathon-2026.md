# AI for Church Hackathon 2026: application plan

Research checked 15 November 2026. This is an internal plan for Jessica and collaborators, not a registration or application. Verify the organizer's current [hackathon page and rules](https://aiforchurch.com/hackathon) and [terms](https://aiforchurch.com/hackathon/legal) before entering.

## Deadline and eligibility

- Submission: **27 November 2026, 11:59 p.m. Eastern Time** (28 November, 10:29 a.m. India time). Enter early; entries lock at the deadline. Organizers announce winners 1 December at noon Eastern.
- Free online event; participants must be 18 or older, and applications are subject to organizer review. One project per team, in one track; each person may belong to only one team. The advertised $2,500 is the total prize pool, not a guaranteed award.
- A new feature inside an existing project is permitted, but **only work completed between 30 October and 27 November 2026 is judged**. Identify earlier work honestly. A new standalone project is preferred; confirm the proposed feature qualifies with the organizer if uncertain.
- Required: project name, description, track and a hosted demo video no longer than five minutes. A live demo and code repository are optional. Do not use real members' journal entries, confessions, messages or other personal data in the demo.
- The organizer may publicly showcase submitted materials. Registration includes partner marketing email consent; the terms say these messages can be unsubscribed from. Entry is free but third-party tool and AI usage may cost money.

## Recommended direction — decide with the team

**Care & Community** is a plausible track: demonstrate a specific, newly built improvement that helps someone seeking support find an appropriate next step. An alternative is **Discipleship & Spiritual Growth** if the new work is principally a Bible-guided reflection. Choose only after reviewing the track descriptions with the team; do not promise a feature is complete or eligible before checking its history.

Candidate: a consent-based journal follow-up that explains one relevant action and connects the person to a real available app feature, such as the confession room, an available gathering or Pay It Forward. When no suitable gathering is available, do not imply one exists. This is a **proposal**, not evidence that the feature has already been built or is eligible. Avoid presenting AI text as a message actually authored by God or as a replacement for pastoral, mental-health or emergency support.

## Work to prepare

- [ ] Agree on team members, captain, track, ownership and prize-sharing; check every participant is eligible and participates in only one team.
- [ ] Audit commits against the build window. Record exactly what predates it, what was completed within it, and who owns or can authorize the assets. Ask the organizer if boundaries are unclear.
- [ ] Select one small, achievable qualifying improvement. Document the user need, acceptance criteria and what it does when services or suitable gatherings are unavailable. Do not build new capabilities solely to satisfy an invented requirement.
- [ ] Test consent and withdrawal before sending personal text to a third-party AI service; show the source and limits of AI assistance. Verify relevant Bible text permissions. Provide human moderation for public interactions.
- [ ] Prepare synthetic, non-sensitive demo accounts and examples; do not expose live accounts, real private entries, personal messages, API keys or private links. Get collaborators' permission before showing their contributions.
- [ ] Test the chosen flow end to end, including no network, permission declined, errors and accessibility. Record observed behavior rather than claiming untested features work.
- [ ] Prepare a short written story: who needs this, what was built during the eligible window, how it works, evidence or feedback we actually have, and how safety and privacy are handled. Do not invent users, testimonials or usage metrics.
- [ ] Record a video under five minutes on a supported public hosting service, confirm it can be viewed by judges, and prepare an optional safe demo link.
- [ ] Re-read the rules and submit before the deadline; save a copy of the submission confirmation. Registration and submission are separate actions and require the team's decision.
